**Q: I feel I don't have enough power from glute and quads. Might it be a reason?**

**Why it fits.** Vertical ratio is bounce divided by stride length. If your glutes, quads, and calves don't produce much push-off, you tend to get a shorter stride and relatively more up-and-down motion, which raises the ratio. Your 1.09 m stride at 172 spm fits that picture, and cadence is the lever you've already been using to compensate.

**Other possible causes:**

- **Height:** Shorter runners naturally have shorter strides, and vertical ratio isn't perfectly comparable across body types.
- **Calf and ankle stiffness:** The calves and Achilles do a lot of the push-off work, and they're often the real weak link.
- **Hip mobility:** Limited hip extension shortens the stride.
- **Running form:** Overstriding, or landing with a lot of vertical bounce, can look like a power problem.

**Signs it's a strength issue:**

- Your legs feel fatigued or quad-dominant late in the run.
- Hills feel disproportionately hard.
- You can't do about 10 controlled single-leg squats to a box, or about 20 single-leg calf raises, per side.
- Strides feel labored rather than springy.

**What to do about it, 2 days a week:**

- **Quads:** Goblet squats, step-ups, and split squats, 3 sets of 6-10.
- **Glutes and hamstrings:** Romanian deadlifts, hip thrusts, and single-leg glute bridges, 3 sets of 6-10.
- **Calves:** Straight-knee and bent-knee calf raises, 3 sets of 8-12, slow and controlled.
- **Power:** Hill sprints, jump rope, and pogo hops once or twice a week.
