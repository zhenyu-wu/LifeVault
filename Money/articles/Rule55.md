The Rule of 55 is an IRS provision that allows workers who leave their job during or after the calendar year they turn 55 to withdraw funds from their current employer's 401(k) or 403(b) without paying the standard 10% early withdrawal penalty, though ordinary income tax still applies. 

Key Requirements

- Separation from Service: You must quit, be laid off, or retire in or after the year you turn 55.
- Target Account: It only applies to the retirement plan of your _most recent_ employer. Funds from older 401(k)s or IRAs do not qualify.
- No Penalty, But Taxable: You avoid the 10% early withdrawal penalty, but your withdrawals will be taxed as ordinary income.
- Qualified Public Safety Workers: Law enforcement officers, firefighters, EMTs, and air traffic controllers are granted an exception and can begin taking penalty-free withdrawals at age 50. 

Critical Rules to Avoid Mistakes

- Keep the Funds in Your Plan: If you roll your 401(k) or 403(b) into an IRA or another employer's plan, you lose eligibility for the Rule of 55. You must leave the funds with your former employer to use the provision.
- Plan Restrictions Vary: Employers are not legally required to permit partial withdrawals. Some plans may force you to withdraw the entire balance as a lump sum, which could push you into a higher tax bracket.
- Check Your Plan Documents: Not all 401(k) plans explicitly feature this provision. You can confirm your eligibility by reviewing your Summary Plan Description (SPD) or contacting your HR department and plan administrator (e.g., Fidelity, Vanguard, or Schwab) directly.
