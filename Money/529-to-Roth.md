The SECURE 2.0 Act allows you to roll over unused 529 funds into a Roth IRA for the plan’s beneficiary without taxes or penalties. The transfer must go directly into a Roth IRA owned by the beneficiary, subject to a lifetime cap of $35,000 and normal annual contribution limits. [1](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/529-rollover-to-roth)

The rollover has strict eligibility requirements and constraints: [1](https://www.invest529.com/move-funds/), [2](https://www.collegecounts529advisor.com/faq-items/what-are-the-limitations-and-restrictions-of-a-529-rollover-to-a-roth-ira/)

- **15-Year Rule:** The 529 account must have been maintained for the designated beneficiary for at least 15 years. [1](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/529-rollover-to-roth)
- **5-Year Rule:** The funds eligible for the transfer must have been in the 529 account (including earnings) for at least 5 years. [1](https://www.collegecounts529advisor.com/faq-items/what-are-the-limitations-and-restrictions-of-a-529-rollover-to-a-roth-ira/), [2](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/529-rollover-to-roth)
- **Annual Limits:** The amount rolled over, combined with any other IRA contributions the beneficiary makes for that tax year, cannot exceed the annual Roth IRA contribution limit (which is $7,500 for individuals under 50, and $8,600 for those 50 and older). [1](https://www.smart529.com/invest/roll-over-unused-529-funds-to-roth-ira-accounts.html)
- **Earned Income:** The beneficiary must have earned income at least equal to the amount being rolled over in that tax year. [1](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/529-to-roth-ira-rollovers-what-to-know)
- **Income Limits Waived:** The typical IRS income limitations that normally restrict high earners from contributing to a Roth IRA do not apply to these rollovers. [1](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/529-to-roth-ira-rollovers-what-to-know), [2](https://www.smart529.com/invest/roll-over-unused-529-funds-to-roth-ira-accounts.html)
- **Beneficiary Control:** Once the funds are transferred, the money belongs entirely to the beneficiary. Many users on [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/Bogleheads/comments/191mage/opening_a_529_account_with_the_intention_of/) agree that this introduces the risk that the beneficiary may choose to withdraw funds prior to retirement.
