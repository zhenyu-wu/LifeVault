#### 8/18 (Tuesday)

We began our trip on 8/17 evening from EWR and landed at KEF on 8/18 morning. With the red-eye flight, I felt a bit dizzy walking out of the airport. After getting our rental car, we went straight to Reykjavik's downtown old harbor area.  We strolled along the river bank, looking over the islands across and the bay. Along the way, we also visited a couple of gift shops and had an expensive lunch of Japanese noodles, before heading to the famous `Hallgrimskirkja` church. Its structure is iconic, with big columns on the two sides of its façade. It is said to be inspired by the basalt rocks common in the country.  Ryan and Melody took the elevator to the top platform to oversee the entire city.

After the church, we drove to our 2nd stop -- the `Perlan` museum, where we took two tours there. In the 1st tour, we walked through an man-made ice cave not very big. In the 2nd tour, we were first shown a movie about the geographic introduction of the island, followed by another 3D-max movie that took us deep down into a volcano. It was feeling really and astonishing.

Leaving the museum, we made a stop to Costco to do some grocery shopping together with a quick diner, before going to our Airbnb.

#### 8/19 (Wednesday)

Today we drove inland to the famous Thingvellir Nation Park. We hiked along the gorge between the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates. The park is also known for serving as an assembly place for the congress back to 1200s for the Norse lived there.  Left the park, we drove a little further to watch the `Strokkur` geyser. The geyser erupts every 5 ~ 10 minutes. Though it doesn't last as long or big as the Old Faithful at Yellowstone, the water gets jetted taller.  The nearby big brother geyser `Geysir` has been inactive since 2000. 

The next stop of the day was a majestic waterfall called `Gullfoss` (`foss` means waterfall in Iceland).  It actually has two waterfalls (upper and lower) with 2.5 km in length and 70 meters in height. When standing close, we can really feel the sheer force and the roaring sound from the rushing water. What's more amazing was that we also saw big rainbows across the waterfall. When we were about the leave, the golden evening sunset cast us across the other side the gorge with our tiny shadows, so that us also became part of the grandiose scene. 

It was really late when we started driving back to Reykjavik, so we made a stop in the middle and had dinner at the campsite restaurant. It served delicious fish & chip, an Icelandic staple made from fresh caught cod.

#### 8/20 (Thursday)

Today we packed up and began driving along the south rim. The first stop was two waterfalls `Seljalandsfoss` and `Gljufabui` close by each other. `Seljalandsfoss` is unique in that it has a cave tucked behind so that we can walk through to see the waterfall from behind.  The waterfall is tall and powerful and it appeared more so by getting so close to its bottom. And because of the rushing water,  we were all wet coming out of the cave.  In contrast, `Gljufabui` is much more peaceful. It's distinctive in that the waterfall is hidden behind a canyon. Visitors need to wade across a shallow stream to go into the canyon to see the whole waterfall.  As there was a long waiting line, we only took some pictures and left.

The next stop on our schedule today was another waterfall called `Skogafoss`.   It is a waterfall that we easily walked directly to its base to see the whole picture. It is big as well, and the mists from the fall creates beautiful rainbows. We then climbed 570 steps up to its top to have an downward view of the waterfall. The climb was a bit workout.

We will spend the night at a town called Vik, and nearby the town is the famous Black Sand Beach. The sand is all black from the volcano ashes, and on the west side the beach there are basalt column cliffs, where lots of puffins like to dwell. That is also a tourist attraction, where people can climb the cliffs at different height to get great pictures. 

We spent the night at a guesthouse in Vik. It was very quiet and clean, with well-stocked food. We had lamb soup in a lava-bread bowl at the nearby soup company.

#### 8/21 (Friday)

Today we continued driving south. The 1st stop was `Fjaðrárgljúfur`, a canyon with a winding river at its bottom and a panoramic view at its top.  Our next stop was the highlight of the trip -- Glacier Lagoon. We saw blue icebergs floating in the lagoon, with occasional sealions coming out of the water in between. There we also met and talked to an interesting Australian, who was touring Iceland on his bicycle! Before our scheduled boat tour, we spent a little time on the diamond beach. Its black sand is decorated with many icicles broken from the icebergs from the lagoon. They are shiny under the sunlight and thus gives the name to the beach.  

In the evening, we got onto a boat tour to get really close to the giant glaciers on the far side of the lagoon. We donned heavy red jumpsuit, boarded a rubber boat, and drove 8 km deep into the lagoon. We saw miles of glacier cliffs, and large icebergs in the lagoon with all kinds of shapes. Due to global warming, more icebergs are breaking from the glaciers at a faster speed each year, and so the lagoon is getting bigger too. Our tour guide is a native Icelandic in his 20s. We asked him many questions and learned a lot. 

On our way to the cabin, we stopped by a hotel restaurant for dinner of lamb-burgers.  When we got to our cabin, it was already very dark. Our cabin is very cute. It has a spacious bunker bed and a kitchenette. I had a good sleep.

#### 8/22 (Saturday)

I woke up in the morning and walked outside our cabin. The scene was surreal: mountains and clouds on the horizon with green pastures taken most of the view, dotted with a few red cabins. Not faraway, a few Icelandic sheep were having their breakfast. When Ryan woke up, he and I walked across a little stream to get very close to them and took a few pictures. 

We started driving to our 1st stop of the day `Skaftafell` & `Svartifoss`.  There we had a brunch before embarked on our hiking. We picked a trail that centered around a waterfall. It was around 3 miles and it took us 2 hours to finish. The waterfall is on a cliff of vertical basalt columns, which reminds me the harpsichord seen in the churches. 

Today we need to drive 4 and half hours back to Reykjavik.  So we skipped another trail to the glacial and began our trip back around 3 pm. In the middle, we stopped by Vik at the guesthouse to return my room key that I forgot to leave there. It was beginning to rain. At around 6 pm, we made another stop at `Dyrholaey`, which is said to be one of the best place to see puffins close by. However, we circled around the deck platforms amid rain and wind, there was no single sign of that bird.   

We were driving in heavy rains for most of the rest of the drive, especially when we were passing some mountain with a lot of thermal steams. When we got back to Reykjavik, that was already 9:15 pm. We found a Chinese restaurant that closes late and had a so-so dinner but quite expensive.

#### 8/23 (Sunday)

This was a day that we changed our direction to visit the `Snaefellsnes` Peninsula. It is called a mini-Iceland because it packs all kinds of geographic features of the country in this small area. The drive was a loop that took 4+ hours.  Along the way, we saw waterfalls, lakes, mountains, glacial, etc. Our first stop was the famous `Kirkjufell` mountain (the Church mountain) due to the TV series `Game of Thrones`.  It is shaped like a hat on one side, and a jagged wedge on the other side.  Our next stop was `Saxholl` Crater. It is a crater formed after a giant volcano eruption 3000 to 4000 years ago. We climbed the iron stairs to the top and looked downwards inside -- not much. Our final stop of the day was another famous spot -- Stone Bridge. It is an arch formed by two pieces of rocks in the sea, quite magnificent.

Today we came back home a bit early and made ourselves a great dinner. We grilled some lamb chops, accompanied with naan / dips, carrots and Icelandic icecreams and drinks . The lamb was tender, delicious!

#### 8/24 (Monday)

Today was our last day of the trip and it began the rain most of the morning, following good weather for almost our entire trip so far. We got up a bit late after several days' hard traveling. Following our schedule, we went to an `Icewear` outlet to do some shopping in the morning and went to Blue Lagoon for the geothermal spa at 2 pm. That place was nothing I had experienced, blue and warm water in a mini-lagoon. The water felt a bit different on the skin but it tasted awful. We also had steam Sauna too. One bad thing was my beloved Pixel 4a got damaged due to the water leak inside the pouch in the pool :-(  We stayed there for about two hours and headed back for another dinner with lamb chops!
